Burning-activity calendar

Counts are satellite fire detections expressed in VIIRS-equivalent pixels (375 m). They are not a number of fires and not burned area. A fire that burns for several days is counted on every satellite pass.

From 2012 the values are observed by VIIRS. Before 2012 they are estimated from MODIS, a coarser sensor, and shown with an 80 % range: about 1 month in 5 falls outside it.

MODIS missed many small fires that VIIRS detects. In an area this size, a month with no value before 2012 means “MODIS detected nothing”, not “nothing burned”. Do not compare how often it burned before and after 2012.

    How to read this
    • Unit: the 0.25° cell. Drawn areas snap to whole cells; an area smaller than one cell becomes that cell.
    • Size tiers: ≥ 100 cells full calendar · 25–99 full, pre-2012 labelled reconstructed · 4–24 baseline 2012–2025, pre-2012 zero = no MODIS detection · 1–3 pre-2012 greyed and excluded unless you toggle them on. Chubut and Santa Cruz: one tier stricter. Tierra del Fuego: 2012 → only.
    • 80 % range: calibrated out of sample per fire-regime region, size class and predicted count, widened in strong ENSO months and in cloudier-than-usual months. Only reconstructed months carry it.
    • Markers: ▲ cloudier than usual at overpass time (the count may be low) · ◆ partly modelled (satellite outage) · hatch: Terra-only era (Nov 2000 – Jun 2002), shown for ≥ 100 cells.
    • Current year: preliminary, near-real-time, NOAA-20/21 converted to S-NPP-equivalent (± 5 %), month to date. Not part of the harmonized record. This is not an alert system: an empty cell does not mean there is no fire.
