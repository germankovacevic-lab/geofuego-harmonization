# Data audit, 14 Nov 2026 (noon) — what we checked, what changed, what it did to the numbers

Context: after the engine was complete (v1, 10:32) we stopped and audited data, method and conclusions before building the app. Three checks found real issues; all fixes are in the scripts and every number below is reproducible.

## Findings

1. **Orbit drift of Terra and Aqua.** Terra's equator-crossing time drifts earlier since 2020 and Aqua's later since 2022. Measured nationally (vegetation class): Aqua/Terra 1.7–1.9 in 2003–2023 → **2.23 (2024), 2.46 (2025)**; VIIRS/Terra ~9 → **11.6 (2025)**. Factors fitted with 2024–2025 would be applied to 2000–2011 when both satellites were nominal. → **Calibration window 2012–2023** (`04_factors.mjs`, `05_loyo.mjs`). Effect on the Terra-only factor: −1.5 % on average.
2. **Satellite outage days inside the series** (`03b_coverage.mjs`; outage = zero national detections while the count expected from another satellite that day is ≥ 8, or a ≥ 3-day zero-run framed by ≥ 5/day): Terra 180 days (Jun 2001: 15, Oct 2022: 13, Mar 2002: 9), Aqua 88 (Apr 2022: 16, Aug 2002: 7), S-NPP 38 (**Aug 2022: 10 — safe-hold in the peak month of the decade's largest fire year**, Jul 2024: 11). Two consequences:
   - **Calibration on paired days only** (Terra, Aqua and S-NPP all operating): columns `Vp,Tp,Ap` in `counts.json`.
   - **Cross-sensor gap filling in the harmonized series** (`06_harmonize.mjs`): VIIRS outage days are filled with f·MODIS of those exact days; Terra/Aqua outage days in 2002–2011 are filled from the other MODIS satellite with the province×month T/A ratio; Terra-only months (2000–mid 2002) are scaled by days. Every filled value carries `gap_fill` and the outage-day counts. Added VIIRS-equivalent hotspots: S-NPP outages 13 243 (2022: +5 635, 2024: +7 086), Terra 5 871, Aqua 5 723, Terra-only era 10 294 (Jun 2001).
3. **A processing bug caught by the re-run:** after downloading the VIIRS SP files for the NOAA-20/21 bridge, `03_aggregate` and `03a_static_sources` iterated every file in the manifest and double-counted VIIRS 2023–2025. Fixed by filtering on sensor. (The committed v1 results predate those downloads and were not affected; the bug would have surfaced on the next run.)

## Addendum (12:00) — region level in the hierarchy
Problem: provinces with little fire (Santa Cruz w = 0.56, Tierra del Fuego w = 0, CABA w = 0) borrowed the missing share of their factor from the **national** mean (≈ 3.0–3.3), dominated by the Chaco and the Litoral (large fires). Santa Cruz and Tierra del Fuego are Patagonian steppe, where small fast grass fires give a higher VIIRS/MODIS ratio (Patagonia annual V/M = 4.03 vs national 3.3).
Change: `lib/regions.js` groups provinces into five fire-regime regions (litoral-nea, noa-chaco-seco, monte-cuyo, pampa, patagonia); the shrinkage is now cell → province → **region** → country (`lib/factors.js`, used by both `04_factors` and `05_loyo`).
Effect (August TA factor): Santa Cruz 5.60 → 5.94, Chubut 4.16 → 4.37, Tierra del Fuego 3.03 → 3.47, CABA 3.03 → 2.79 (pampa); provinces with w ≈ 1 unchanged (< 1 %). ## Addendum (12:30) — does the ratio depend on the ENSO phase? (team hypothesis, tested)
Hypothesis: dry (La Niña) years burn more small, fragmented fire that VIIRS sees and MODIS misses → higher ratio; a phase-blind factor would bias reconstructed La Niña years (2007–08, 2008–09, 2010–11). `scripts/05e_enso.mjs`, ONI from NOAA CPC.
- **Test 1, correlation:** log(V/M) vs ONI, national: annual r = −0.36, monthly residual (seasonality removed) r = −0.37 (n = 144, p < 0.001). Pampa −0.46, litoral −0.37 monthly, Patagonia/Cuyo ≈ 0. Sign as hypothesised: La Niña → higher ratio. Real, but modest (≈ 13 % of the interannual variance).
- **Test 2, leave-one-phase-out:** fitted on the other phases, La Niña years (2020–22) are predicted at pred/obs **0.918** (province MAPE 11.9 %) vs 0.949 (9.3 %) when each year is left out alone; El Niño years 0.988 vs 0.987; neutral 1.021 vs 0.996. So a factor fitted without any La Niña year under-predicts La Niña by ~8 %; with the phase represented in the calibration (as it is: 3 Niña, 3 Niño, 6 neutral), the residual phase bias is ~5 %.
- **Test 3, ENSO-conditioned factor** log f = log f_base + β·ONI(month): β per region −0.015…−0.084 (per ONI unit), single national β = −0.025. LOYO: province MAPE 10.6 % → 11.0 % (regional β) / 10.8 % (national β); annual pred/obs rms deviation 0.037 → 0.029 (2022: 0.901 → 0.933). External check on the reconstructed years (MCD64A1): continuity 0.971 → 0.971, r(log) 0.857 → 0.847. **No gain against independent data.**
- **Decision:** the effect is real but small (≈ ±4 % in strong years) and conditioning does not improve the series where it can be checked. Not adopted as a correction (`06_harmonize.mjs --enso-beta` kept as an option, default off). Declared as a known phase-dependent uncertainty of the reconstructed years; strong-ONI months carry it in the band (see §2.4 of the design).

## Addendum (13:40) — additive term for months where MODIS saw nothing: tested, not adopted

## Addendum (14:30) — cloud obscuration, measured (ERA5)
Why: MODIS and VIIRS detect fire in the mid/thermal infrared, which cloud blocks (only microwaves see through cloud). A cloudy month reads as a quiet month, and Terra (10:30) and Aqua/VIIRS (13:30) see different clouds. Data: ERA5 monthly cloud fraction **by hour of day** at 0.25° (CDS; `01d_fetch_era5_cloud.py`, `03c_cloud_cells.py`: cloud at the Terra, 13:30 and night overpasses per cell-month, with anomalies vs 2000–2025 climatology) and ERA5 monthly precipitation (`01e`, `03d`). Tests in `05f_cloud.mjs`:
- **A (does cloud bias the factor?)** LOYO residual log(V/P) per province-month vs the differential cloud anomaly (13:30 minus 10:30): **r = 0.02**. Vs the absolute 13:30 anomaly: r = −0.13, slope −0.47. Cloud does not bias the MODIS→VIIRS conversion; the harmonized series is as cloud-affected as the observed one, no more.
- **B (does cloud lower the counts?)** log(count / province-month climatology) vs cloud anomaly at the overpass: r = −0.46 (VIIRS), −0.42 (MODIS); slope ≈ −4.2 to −4.6 per unit cloud fraction. Confounded: cloudy months are rainy months with less fire (corr(cloud, precip) = 0.55–0.59).
- **C (cloud conditional on rain)** OLS log(count/clim) ~ cloud anomaly + precipitation anomaly: cloud slope **−3.1 (VIIRS), −2.8 (MODIS)** per unit cloud fraction, precipitation −0.19/−0.16 per mm/day, R² 0.21–0.27. A +0.10 cloud anomaly at the overpass ≈ **−26 % detections** after accounting for rain; still an upper bound for pure obscuration (cloud also tracks humidity). Equal for both sensors → consistent with test A.
Product decision: counts are **not corrected** (part of the effect is real fire weather). Every cell-month and province-month gets `cloud_anom` (at its overpass) and `cloud_underdetection` = 1 − exp(−3·Δ) when Δ > +0.05; in reconstructed months the upper bound of the 80 % interval is widened by exp(3·Δ); in observed months the flag tells the user the count is likely a lower bound. `cells.json` carries the flagged months (`cl`) for the app.

## Addendum (14:25) — prediction interval per fire-regime region: tested out-of-sample, adopted
Why: the pooled count-binned interval covered 74 % of Neuquén's, 66 % of Chubut's and 33 % of Tierra del Fuego's out-of-sample province-months (target 80 %), and 74 % of Patagonian cell-months. The pooled bins are dominated by the Chaco and the Litoral (large fires, small residuals); Patagonian residuals are wider and more biased (bin 15–40: p10 −1.69 vs −1.18 pooled, median −0.40 vs −0.20).
Method (`05_loyo.mjs`): the same signed p10/p90 bins by predicted count, fitted per region of `lib/regions.js` over the cell- and province-level residuals of that region (country stays pooled; bins with < 30 residuals fall back to pooled — the 1000+ bins of Patagonia and Cuyo, 3000+ of Pampa). Two variants compared: **A** region (adopted) and **B** region × level (province residuals separate from cell residuals).
Honest test: the coverage published until now was in-sample for the band (the same residuals calibrate the quantiles and measure the coverage), so any regional variant would read ≈ 80 % by construction. The coverage of the three bands is now measured **out-of-sample for the band itself**: quantiles from 11 years of residuals, coverage on the 12th, all 12 folds. For the pooled band the two numbers coincide (country 90 / province 81 / cell 80 %), so the published figures stand.

## Addendum (15:40) — the FIRMS MODIS `type` flag is not homogeneous across archive versions: measured, fixed, adopted
Found by peer review round 3 (`docs/peer_review_round3_judge.md` §4.2) and verified on the raw files. The MODIS country archives carry `version` 6.2 up to **2017**, 6.03 for **2018**–2022 and 61.03 for 2023–2025 (README said 2018/2019: corrected). The share of `type = 2` (static land source) among confident detections is 0.4–1.8 % through 2017 and 2.3–4.9 % from 2018, and the same fixed sites flip from type 0 to type 2 at the boundary. We kept `type == 0` as "vegetation fire", so the vegetation population changed in the middle of the calibration window (6 years each side), and the whole reconstructed period 2000–2011 belongs to the pre-2018 population.

What those detections are (raw files, 2012–2017 MODIS type-0 detections inside the 1 001 cells that carry ≥ 3 type-2 detections in ≥ 2 years of 2018–2025, vs all other type-0 detections): VIIRS same-day co-location within the 3 × 3 cell neighbourhood **11 % vs 76 %**; daytime **97 % vs 86 %**; median FRP **8.1 vs 21.6 MW**; peak months **Dec–Feb vs Jul–Oct**; in Dec–Feb the co-location is **1.5 %**. Post-2018 type-2 detections in the same cells: 12 % co-location, 93 % day, 8.3 MW, Dec–Feb. Same population. These are not fires VIIRS misses: they are hot bare surfaces in summer (Buenos Aires, Santa Fe, Mendoza, Córdoba, San Juan) that the 1 km MODIS algorithm flags and the 375 m VIIRS algorithm rejects, and that NASA started labelling as static sources with version 6.03. The real fire that occurs in those cells is still labelled type 0 after 2018 (1 567 detections, 60 % co-location) and VIIRS sees 1 200–2 000 hotspots per year there.

Rule 3 of the static class (`03a_static_sources.mjs`): 0.02° cells with ≥ 3 MODIS type-2 detections in ≥ 2 distinct years of 2018–2025, **not dilated** (the reviewer's one-cell dilation would also have swallowed the Tucumán sugar-cane fields, which are recurrent real fire: 5 739 cells, 26 % of Tucumán's MODIS). 955 new cells (Buenos Aires 234, Mendoza 127, Santa Fe 95, Córdoba 80, Tucumán 74, San Juan 53); 46 were already in the class. The class is now **1 628 cells**, 3.0 % of kept hotspots. The cells apply to both sensors and all years, like the other two rules, so the vegetation population is the same before and after 2018. In `03_aggregate.mjs` MODIS type-2 detections are now counted in the static columns instead of dropped (types 1 and 3 are still dropped).
